Unnayan Nidhi · System conversion plan

From thirteen spreadsheets to one ledger

A plan for replacing the Google Sheets operation with a web-based core banking system — without ever putting a member's balance at risk.

Planning stage · no code written Read from the live Drive folder, 16 Aug 2026 ~208 members · 110 columns · 11 officers
01

What runs the business today

Thirteen live spreadsheets. One master, eleven per-officer collection workspaces, and a three-megabyte collection abstract. Officers work in their own file, and the data is merged back to master by hand.

FileSizeRole
UNNAYAN_CORE_MASTER1.6 MBThe book of record — one row per customer, 110 columns
Collection_Abstract3.0 MBThe largest file in the system — collection history
11 × *_Collection_Workspace~230–410 KB eachOne forked workspace per officer: GS, CS, RS, MS, KM, CD, RG, SDS, S2, S3, AG
6 × "Copy of ... backup"variousDated manual snapshots, Feb–Apr 2026 — serving as version control

Those six backup copies are the most revealing thing in the folder, because of what they mean rather than what they contain. Manual dated copies are what a system produces when it has no transaction log and no undo. You take a snapshot before a risky merge because if the merge goes wrong there is no other way back.

The last one is named Copy of UNNAYAN_CORE_MASTER for DB. The intent to move to a database is already there. This is the route.

Worth saying plainly

The domain logic in that master sheet is genuinely good — arrears ageing, overdue-interest capping with grace days and discounts, NPA classification, a two-axis credit grade with consistency percentages. None of it is being discarded. The problem is not the thinking. It is that a spreadsheet cannot enforce the thinking, cannot hold more than one loan per member, and cannot tell you who took the cash.

02

Five problems that are already costing money

Structural

One row cannot hold one customer's real life

A row has exactly one loan account number, one deposit account number, one FD. It cannot represent a second concurrent loan, a deposit closed in 2023 and reopened in 2025, or a member who leaves and rejoins.

Integrity

Balances are formulas, not facts

There is no underlying list of individual payments that a balance is computed from and can be re-added against. If a figure is wrong, the only fix is to overwrite it — the one operation that leaves no trace.

Concurrency

Eleven workspaces are eleven forks

Bringing them back to master is a manual merge, with three silent failure modes: a lost update, a double-post, or a workspace that quietly stops matching and is not noticed for weeks.

Accountability

No identity, so no audit

A User Email column is not authentication. Sheets history tells you a cell changed — not that ₹250 was accepted from a named member by officer GS at 10:47 on the doorstep.

Data quality

Nothing is validated on entry

The officer field carries values like RS, NPA, GS., CD, CS and MS Close — officer, status and stray punctuation sharing one ungated field. Every report built on it has to guess.

Consequence

True exposure per member is invisible

The workarounds are visible in the live data: a customer recorded as TARAK KUNDU 1 is a human being cut in half so a second account could get its own row. For a lender, that is the most expensive kind of blindness.

From the live file timestamps

On 16 August 2026, six workspaces were modified between 19:31 and 19:42 while master was last touched at 18:39. At that moment master was stale against six different files simultaneously. That is the normal operating state, not an anomaly. Separately, the RG, MS, S2 and S3 workspaces have not been modified since May–June 2026 — either those officers are inactive, or their work has not reached master in months.

03

The one decision everything follows from

Stop storing balances. Store every rupee that moves, and derive the balance from that.

Today a deposit balance is a number in a cell. In the new system there is no such cell. There is an append-only list of every transaction, and the balance is the sum of that list. When a member asks why their balance is ₹5,990, the system answers with the forty-seven receipts that add up to it.

This single change dissolves most of the five problems at once:

  • Merge conflicts stop existing. Two officers posting collections are two inserts. Inserts never conflict; only overwrites do.
  • The dated backup copies become unnecessary, because nothing is ever overwritten and any past state is a query with a date filter.
  • Audit becomes free rather than a feature bolted on — the log is the audit trail.
  • Double-posting becomes impossible, because each entry carries a client-generated idempotency key and the second insert of the same key is rejected.

04

How 110 columns become a real data model

The roughly forty computed columns in master do not become table columns. They become SQL views, so they can never disagree with the ledger.

Products are versioned by date, so when you change the FD rate next year, existing deposits keep the rate they were opened under. A spreadsheet cannot do this, and it is essential for correctness.

05

Two things the field work demands

The collection app must work with no signal at all

Officers collect at doorsteps in Khosalpur, Aranghata, Jugolbari, Narayanpur, Babupara, Shikri and Nimtala. An app that needs connectivity to record a collection will fail at the exact moment cash changes hands — and staff will fall back to a paper notebook, recreating the merge problem you are trying to escape.

So the app pre-loads the officer's whole round each morning on office Wi-Fi, records collections into a local queue with no network, issues the member a receipt immediately from a reserved number block, and syncs when it can. The rule that makes this safe: a collection is only ever an append. An officer can add a receipt offline; an officer can never edit or delete one. Corrections are supervisor reversals made in the office. That restriction is precisely what allows conflict-free sync.

Cash must be counted and reconciled every day

This does not exist today and is the highest-value operational addition. At end of day the app shows the officer what the system says they collected. The officer enters the physical cash by denomination. If the two agree, they hand over and a supervisor accepts. If they differ, the difference is recorded as a named shortage or excess against that officer on that date — never silently absorbed.

Cash that is counted daily and reconciled to a receipt list is cash that cannot quietly leak. For a doorstep-collection lender, this is the single most important internal control there is.

06

Migrating to a reconciled zero

Not "load the data and start using it" — load it, then prove that every member's balance in the new system equals their balance in the sheet, member by member, before anyone relies on it.

Zero unexplained differences. Not close enough. A migration that silently changes one member's balance destroys trust that took years to build, and in a doorstep business trust is the business.

The recommendation is to migrate opening balances at a cut-off date rather than reconstructing full history at go-live: fast, low-risk, and reconciliation becomes a single number per account. Historical transactions can be back-loaded from Collection_Abstract afterwards as a non-blocking improvement, rather than as a launch risk. The old sheets are then frozen read-only — kept as archive, never deleted, and never editable again.

Both systems run in parallel for one full collection cycle, because the daily and weekly frequencies both need to complete. Daily totals are compared each evening and every difference investigated same-day. It is double work for a month, and it is the cheapest insurance available.

07

Sequence

Ordered so the highest-risk, highest-value change — collections stop being merged by hand — happens as early as possible.

  1. Phase 0 Decide and prepareAnswer the open questions, profile the data, confirm compliance thresholds 1–2 weeks
  2. Phase 1 FoundationSchema, constraints, auth, migration scripts and the reconciliation harness 3–4 weeks
  3. Phase 2 Collection app — the core of the projectOffline PWA, receipts, day-book, piloted with one officer first 4–5 weeks
  4. Phase 3 Office appOnboarding, loan origination, reversals, reports, member statements 4–5 weeks
  5. Phase 4 AutomationInterest accrual, penalties, NPA ageing, re-scoring, WhatsApp reminders 2–3 weeks
  6. Phase 5 Parallel run and cutoverBounded by a real collection cycle, not by effort — cannot be compressed 4–5 weeks

Roughly five to seven months to full cutover with a small team working steadily. Recurring infrastructure cost at this scale is small — tens of dollars a month for managed Postgres and hosting. The real cost is development time, and the real saving is the hours now spent merging workspaces and hunting differences, plus the losses that daily cash reconciliation prevents.

08

What I need from you before Phase 1

Fifteen questions are listed in the repository. These four change the design most:

  1. Where will you be in three years? At ~200 members almost any design works. At 5,000 across multiple branches, branch-scoping has to be in the schema from day one rather than retrofitted.
  2. What is Collection_Abstract? It is your largest file. If it holds clean transaction-level history it is the key to full history and to validating the migration. If it is a derived summary, its role is much smaller.
  3. Opening balances, or full history? Fast and low-risk, versus complete and slower. My recommendation is opening balances now, back-load later.
  4. What are the actual rules? The interest, overdue-capping and credit-scoring logic lives in formulas and in your head. It needs to be stated plainly before it can be encoded — deposit accrual, what triggers an accrual reversal, the overdue rate and cap, who may grant a discount and up to what limit, and exactly when a loan becomes NPA.
